In short

What is SEA Audit?

SEA Audit is an independent audit service for supported exempt accommodation (SEA) and transitional supported housing in England. We review resident support files (assessments, support plans, risk assessments, session notes and reviews) against your own standards and the National Supported Housing Standards, then report what is working, what is missing and exactly how to fix it.

Our approach

Every finding names the document, the date and what good looks like. Every file gets a clear rating and the reason for it. Strengths are named as specifically as gaps, because the point is improvement.

Why now

Licensing is coming. Evidence is what will count.

Supported exempt accommodation is moving to national standards and local licences. Providers who can show their support, file by file, will be ready for it.

01

National standards, local licences

The Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023 lets government set National Supported Housing Standards and require a local licence to run supported exempt accommodation. Meeting the standards becomes a licence condition.

02

Housing Benefit tied to the licence

In its April 2026 response, government confirmed it will amend Housing Benefit regulations so that enhanced Housing Benefit in England is linked to the new licensing regime.

03

A short window to prepare

Government expects to consult on draft regulations in late 2026, with licensing expected from 2027. Local supported housing strategies are due by 31 March 2027.

What we check

Ten documents. One standard.

Scroll to move through the file

  1. 01

    Referral form

    Why the resident was referred and by whom. Every question answered, signed and dated.

  2. 02

    Admission form

    The start of the stay and what the resident was told, signed on the move-in day.

  3. 03

    Licence agreement

    The right landlord or agent name, address and rent, signed by both parties.

  4. 04

    Needs assessment

    Completed within four weeks of move-in and kept current as things change.

  5. 05

    Support plan

    Goals that follow from assessed needs, specific to the person and agreed with them.

  6. 06

    Risk assessment

    Each risk scored and described, consistent with the rest of the file.

  7. 07

    Risk management plans

    A current, dated plan for every risk that scores high enough to need one.

  8. 08

    Session notes

    Regular, dated, linked to a goal, showing the support given and the progress made.

  9. 09

    Reviews

    At 3, 6 and 9 months, signed by the resident and staff, with goals updated.

  10. 10

    Exit or move-on form

    Completed whenever a resident leaves, with the outcome and forwarding details.

Questions

Good to know.

Short answers to what providers ask most. Anything else, ask us on the call.

What does SEA Audit do?[[pm]]

We audit resident support files in supported exempt accommodation and transitional supported housing. Each file is checked against a set checklist, rated Gold, Green, Amber or Red with a reason, and returned with evidence, strengths and practical next steps.

What does SEA stand for?[[pm]]

Supported exempt accommodation: supported housing where Housing Benefit can be paid above normal Local Housing Allowance levels because the landlord, or someone acting for it, also provides care, support or supervision.

Who do you audit for?[[pm]]

Registered Providers who work with managing agents to deliver support, and managing agents or support providers who want an independent check of their own files.

Do you visit properties?[[pm]]

No. Our audits are desktop reviews of resident records, carried out remotely or at your office. We don't carry out property inspections.

Who decides the rating?[[pm]]

An experienced auditor. Our audit system makes sure every file is checked the same way, but every judgement and every grade is made by a person, with the reason written down.

How often should resident files be audited?[[pm]]

Most audit rounds run every six months and cover the records for that review period. Monitoring adds lighter sampling in between, so issues are caught early.

Will the feedback be harsh?[[pm]]

It will be honest and specific, but the aim is to help teams improve. Strengths are named alongside issues, and every issue comes with an example of what good looks like.
