Quick answers

The questions we are asked most.

Short, sourced answers. Follow the guides for the detail.

What is supported exempt accommodation?

Supported exempt accommodation is housing provided by a housing association, registered charity, voluntary organisation or certain councils, where that body or someone acting for it also provides the resident with care, support or supervision. It is "exempt" because Housing Benefit can be paid above normal Local Housing Allowance limits.9

What is the difference between exempt and specified accommodation?

Specified accommodation is the wider group of supported housing whose rent is still met through Housing Benefit rather than Universal Credit. Exempt accommodation is one type within it. The new licensing regime will apply to supported exempt accommodation that matches the specified accommodation definition.2

Who will need a supported housing licence?

The person managing or in control of supported exempt accommodation will need a licence for each licensing district they operate in. The licence will list every address they are authorised to run. Some services are exempt, including almshouses, commissioned domestic abuse services and certain council-managed accommodation.2

When does supported housing licensing start?

No start date has been set. Government expects to consult on draft regulations in late 2026, and licensing is expected to begin at some point in 2027.2,3

What are the National Supported Housing Standards?

They are national standards for the support provided in supported housing in England, built on five principles: person-centred, respectful, safe and responsive, effective, and well-led. The six standards cover person-centred support, empowerment, environment, staff and safeguarding, local need, and a statement of purpose.2

Will licensing affect Housing Benefit?

Yes. Government will amend Housing Benefit regulations so that enhanced Housing Benefit in England is linked to the licensing regime. Plans for new definitions of care, support and supervision have been paused.2,3

What is a managing agent in supported housing?

A managing agent is an organisation that manages supported housing day to day, often in properties leased from a Registered Provider, and frequently delivers or arranges the support. The Registered Provider stays accountable as landlord.

What is transitional supported housing?

Transitional supported housing offers support for a set period, generally six months to two years, often after homelessness, domestic abuse or another crisis. The aim is to help residents move on to independent living.7

Key dates

How we got here, and what comes next.

From a non-statutory statement of expectations to a national licensing regime.

  1. 20 Oct 2020

    National Statement of Expectations

    Government sets out non-statutory guidance on what good supported housing looks like.6

  2. Mar 2021

    Birmingham charter and quality standards

    Birmingham, with more than 20,000 units of exempt accommodation, launches a residents' charter and provider quality standards.10

  3. Oct 2022

    Select committee: "a complete mess"

    MPs call for national standards and council enforcement powers.8

  4. 2022 to 2025

    Supported Housing Improvement Programme

    £20 million for councils to inspect, review support and scrutinise claims.7

  5. 29 Jun 2023

    The Act receives Royal Assent

    Powers for national standards, local licensing and local strategies.1

  6. 16 Apr 2026

    Government response published

    Standards, licence conditions and the Housing Benefit link confirmed.2

  7. Late 2026

    Consultation on draft regulations

    Expected before regulations are laid in Parliament.2

  8. 31 Mar 2027

    Local supported housing strategies due

    Every local housing authority in England must publish one.3

  9. 2027

    Licensing expected to begin

    Date to be confirmed in regulations.3

Glossary

Supported housing terms, defined.

22 terms you will meet in contracts, audits and the new regulations.

Supported exempt accommodation (SEA)
Supported housing that qualifies as exempt accommodation for Housing Benefit, so rent can be paid above Local Housing Allowance levels.
Exempt accommodation
Housing Benefit term for accommodation provided by a housing association, registered charity, voluntary organisation or certain councils that also provide the resident with care, support or supervision.
Specified accommodation
The wider group of supported housing whose housing costs are met through Housing Benefit rather than Universal Credit. Exempt accommodation sits within it.
Enhanced Housing Benefit
Housing Benefit paid above Local Housing Allowance levels for exempt accommodation. It will be linked to holding a licence.
Registered Provider (RP)
A social landlord registered with the Regulator of Social Housing, such as a housing association.
Managing agent
An organisation that manages supported housing day to day, often in homes leased from a Registered Provider, and often delivers the support.
Support provider
The organisation that delivers the care, support or supervision to residents. It may or may not be the landlord.
Licence holder
Under the new regime, the person managing or in control of supported housing in a licensing district. They must pass a fit and proper person test.
Licensing authority
The local housing authority that will issue supported housing licences for its district.
Fit and proper person test
A check on whether a licence applicant is suitable. It will extend to directors, partners and nominated individuals.
National Supported Housing Standards
National standards for the support provided in supported housing in England. Meeting them will be a licence condition.
National Statement of Expectations
Non-statutory government guidance from October 2020 on what good supported housing looks like.
Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023
The Act giving government powers to set national standards, introduce licensing and require local supported housing strategies.
Local supported housing strategy
A plan each local housing authority in England must publish by 31 March 2027, covering current supply and future need.
Care, support or supervision
The help a resident must receive for accommodation to be exempt. Plans to define it more tightly have been paused.
Transitional supported housing
Supported housing for a set period, generally six months to two years, aimed at helping residents move on to independent living.
Move-on
The planned move from supported housing into more independent accommodation, and the support that prepares for it.
Support needs assessment
A record of a resident's support needs, completed after move-in and kept current. Licensing will require one within four weeks of move-in.
Support plan
The goals agreed with a resident to meet their assessed needs, and how they will be supported to reach them.
Risk assessment
A record of risks to and from a resident, each scored, usually by likelihood and severity, and reviewed regularly.
Risk management plan (RMP)
The actions agreed to reduce a risk that scores high enough to need one.
Support session
A recorded contact where support is delivered, ideally linked to a goal in the support plan.
