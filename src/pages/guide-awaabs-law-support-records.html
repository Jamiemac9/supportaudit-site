Awaab's Law Phase 2:when a support note starts the clock.

From 30 November 2026 a hazard a resident mentions in a support session can start a legal deadline. This guide covers who is in scope, when the clock starts, and how support teams should log what they hear.

Key takeaways

  • [[tick]]Awaab's Law applies to homes let by a registered provider under a tenancy. It does not apply to housing occupied under a licence.
  • [[tick]]From 30 November 2026 it covers seven more hazard types, including excess cold, electrical hazards and pests.
  • [[tick]]The government guidance says a managing agent becoming aware of a hazard is likely to count as the landlord becoming aware.
  • [[tick]]So a hazard a resident raises in a support session should be logged and passed on the same day, with the date and time.

Does Awaab's Law apply to your scheme?

Ask two questions. First, is the landlord a registered provider of social housing? Awaab's Law sits in social housing tenancies, so it applies to registered providers. Second, is the resident on a tenancy or a licence? The Phase 2 guidance includes supported accommodation occupied under a tenancy, and says the law does not apply to housing occupied under a licence.

Many supported exempt accommodation schemes use licences. Many lease-based schemes have a registered provider as the landlord and a managing agent delivering housing management and support. The answer can differ from one scheme to the next, so check the actual occupancy agreements rather than assuming.

If a scheme is out of scope, the property still has to be safe. Councils can still act on hazards under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and the government has said Awaab's Law will be extended to the private rented sector later.

When does the clock start?

The deadlines run from when the landlord becomes aware of a potential hazard. The guidance lists the ways that can happen, including a tenant or their representative raising it, a routine inspection, or a third party reporting it. Where an employee is told about a hazard, the landlord is treated as aware at that point. The guidance also says a managing agent becoming aware is likely to establish awareness.

In practice that means a resident telling their support worker the heating has failed, or a socket is sparking, may already count. If the note sits in the support file and never reaches the repairs log, the deadline is running and nobody is acting on it.

The Phase 2 timescales

HazardWhat the landlord must do
EmergencyInvestigate and make safe within 24 hours of becoming aware
SignificantInvestigate within 10 working days, then make safe within 5 working days of the investigation ending
Further workStart within 5 working days of the investigation, or as soon as reasonably practicable and within 12 weeks
Written summaryGive the tenant a written summary within 3 working days of the investigation ending, unless all works are already done

The new hazard types from 30 November 2026 are excess cold, excess heat, falls, structural collapse and falling elements, fire and explosion, electrical hazards, and domestic and personal hygiene, including pests. Damp and mould, and all emergency hazards, have been covered since 27 October 2025.

How support staff should log a hazard

The guidance asks landlords to record engagement, investigations and communications with tenants, including reports from third parties, and to log every attempt to get access. Support files are often where the first report lands, so the support record should show five things:

  1. What the resident said, in their words where possible.
  2. The date and time they said it.
  3. Who it was passed to, how, and when. A repair reference number is the best evidence.
  4. What the resident was told would happen next.
  5. A follow-up note at the next session: was it fixed, and is the resident safe in the meantime?

Write the hazard in the session note even when the session was about something else. An auditor or inspector reading the file later should be able to match every hazard in the support notes to an entry in the repairs log.

Self-check: would your files stand up?

  • Do you know which of your residents are on tenancies and which are on licences?
  • Do support staff know that a hazard they hear about may start a legal deadline?
  • Is there one place to report a hazard, and does it give back a reference?
  • Pick ten recent session notes. Does every hazard mentioned have a matching repair entry?
  • Does the managing agent agreement say who logs hazards and how fast?
