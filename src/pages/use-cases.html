Nine situations where Registered Providers, managing agents and support providers use SEA Audit, what we do in each, and what you get at the end.

In short

When do providers need an independent audit?

An independent audit is most useful at moments of change or scrutiny, when you need evidence rather than assurance:

  1. 1Before scrutiny: licensing, a council review, a Housing Benefit query or an RP audit round.
  2. 2At a change: a new managing agent, a lease renewal or a move into transitional housing.
  3. 3After a concern: a complaint, safeguarding issue or serious incident.
  4. 4As routine: six-monthly rounds and monitoring, so standards hold all year.

Registered Providers

Overseeing several managing agents

You lease homes to more than one managing agent, each with its own forms, habits and standards.

Recommended: Audit Rounds

The situation

Reports from each agent look different, so it is hard to compare them or know where the real risks sit.

What we do

Audit every file in scope against one checklist and one rating key, whichever agent holds it.

What you get

Like-for-like ratings across agents, the weakest files first, and a record of improvement over time.

Managing agents and providers

Getting ready for supported housing licensing

Licensing is expected from 2027, and meeting the National Supported Housing Standards will be a licence condition.

Recommended: Mock Audit

The situation

You believe your files are in order but have never had them checked independently against the new standards.

What we do

A Mock Audit of a sample of files, mapped to the four standards that can be evidenced from resident records.

What you get

A clear list of gaps and fixes while there is still time, and a re-audit to show the improvement.

Registered Providers

Onboarding a new managing agent

A new arrangement starts with good intentions and very little shared evidence.

Recommended: Baseline audit, then Audit Rounds

The situation

You need to know early whether the agent's recording meets your standards, not at the first problem.

What we do

A baseline audit in the first months, with clear guidance on your forms and what good looks like.

What you get

Expectations set on evidence, early fixes, and a starting point to measure future rounds against.

Registered Providers

Renewing or reviewing a lease

A lease or management agreement is up for renewal, and the decision needs to rest on more than relationships.

Recommended: Audit Rounds or a one-off review

The situation

You need an objective view of how support has been delivered across the term.

What we do

Review current files and, where you have them, trends from earlier rounds.

What you get

An evidence base for renewal, conditions for improvement, or a documented reason to change course.

After a complaint, safeguarding concern or incident

Something has gone wrong, and you need to understand what the records show, calmly and fairly.

Recommended: Targeted file review

The situation

Questions are being asked about one resident or one service, and memories differ.

What we do

An independent review of the relevant files, reporting only what is documented and when.

What you get

A factual timeline, the gaps in recording, and practical changes to prevent a repeat.

Registered ProvidersManaging agents and providers

Moving from exempt into transitional housing

Residents are moving from supported exempt accommodation into a transitional pathway, usually a stay of six months to two years.

Recommended: Audit Rounds with move-on focus

The situation

Records need to show progress towards independence, not only that support took place.

What we do

Audit the move-on plan, the goals that lead to it and the handover between services.

What you get

Evidence that each move was planned and supported, and fewer residents falling between services.

Registered Providers

Board and governance assurance

Your board is accountable for services delivered in your name, including by managing agents.

Recommended: Audit Rounds with executive summary

The situation

Board papers rely on agents' own reporting, with little independent challenge.

What we do

Turn each audit round into a short board summary: ratings, trends, risks and open actions.

What you get

Independent assurance your board can rely on and question.

Managing agents and providers

Raising the standard of recording across a team

Your support workers do good work, but the notes don't always show it.

Recommended: Mock Audit, then Monitoring

The situation

Recording varies between staff, and good practice isn't shared.

What we do

Use real findings, anonymised, to show what good looks like for each document and goal.

What you get

Named strengths to build on, clear examples for staff, and steadier ratings over time.

Managing agents and providers

Evidencing support for Housing Benefit scrutiny

Councils are looking more closely at whether exempt claims show real care, support or supervision.

Recommended: Mock Audit

The situation

A council has asked for evidence of the support behind your claims, or you expect it will.

What we do

Check that each file shows assessed needs, a plan to meet them and support actually delivered.

What you get

Files that answer the question before it is asked, and a clear view of any that don't.
